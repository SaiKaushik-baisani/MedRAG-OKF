## Overview
Fatigue is a feeling of constant tiredness or lack of energy that does not improve with rest. Occasional tiredness is normal, but fatigue that lasts for more than two weeks should be discussed with a doctor.

## Common causes
Persistent fatigue has many possible causes. Common ones include poor or insufficient sleep, iron deficiency anemia, an underactive thyroid, vitamin D or vitamin B12 deficiency, and diabetes with high blood sugar. Depression and anxiety, chronic stress, infections such as flu or dengue, and some medicines can also cause tiredness. Sleep apnea, long-term heart or kidney disease, and heavy alcohol use are other recognised causes.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if tiredness lasts more than two weeks, if it interferes with daily life, or if it comes with unexplained weight loss, fever, night sweats, shortness of breath, pale skin, or feeling very thirsty and passing a lot of urine. Seek urgent care for chest pain, fainting, or severe breathlessness.

## Tests a doctor may order
A doctor may check a full blood count for anemia, thyroid function tests, fasting blood glucose or HbA1c, vitamin D and vitamin B12 levels, and kidney and liver function.

## Self-care measures
Keep a regular sleep schedule of seven to nine hours, eat balanced meals, drink enough water, and do regular moderate exercise such as a daily walk. Limit alcohol and caffeine late in the day. Gradually increasing activity often helps more than resting all day.
