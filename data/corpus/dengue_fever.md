## Overview
Dengue is a viral infection spread by the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, which bite mainly during the day. It is common in tropical and subtropical regions including India, especially during and after the monsoon.

## Symptoms
Most people have mild or no symptoms. Typical dengue causes sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting and a skin rash. Symptoms usually appear four to ten days after the bite and last two to seven days.

## Warning signs of severe dengue
Warning signs usually appear as the fever falls, around days three to seven. They include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding from the gums or nose, blood in vomit or stool, rapid breathing, restlessness or extreme tiredness. These need immediate hospital care.

## Treatment
There is no specific antiviral treatment. Care involves rest and plenty of fluids, and paracetamol for fever and pain. Aspirin and ibuprofen should be avoided because they can increase the risk of bleeding. A doctor may monitor blood platelet counts and hydration.

## Prevention
Prevent mosquito bites using repellents, long sleeves and bed nets. Remove standing water from containers, coolers, flower pots and tyres where Aedes mosquitoes breed. A dengue vaccine is available in some countries for specific groups, as advised by health authorities.
