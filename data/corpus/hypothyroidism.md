## Overview
Hypothyroidism means the thyroid gland, a small gland in the neck, does not make enough thyroid hormone. This slows down the body's metabolism. It is more common in women and in people over 60.

## Symptoms
Symptoms develop slowly and include tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold, constipation, dry skin, hair thinning, muscle aches, a slow heartbeat, low mood and heavy periods. A swelling in the neck called a goitre may appear.

## Causes
The most common cause is autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's disease). Other causes include iodine deficiency, previous thyroid surgery or radioiodine treatment, and some medicines.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis uses a blood test for thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) and free thyroxine (T4). A high TSH with low T4 indicates an underactive thyroid.

## Treatment
Treatment is a daily tablet of levothyroxine, a synthetic thyroid hormone, usually taken on an empty stomach. The dose is adjusted using blood tests. Treatment is usually lifelong and should not be stopped without medical advice.

## Follow-up
Thyroid levels are checked every few months at first and then about once a year. Untreated hypothyroidism can lead to high cholesterol, heart problems and, rarely, a serious condition called myxoedema coma.
