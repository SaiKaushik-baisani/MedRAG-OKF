## Overview
High blood pressure, or hypertension, means the force of blood against artery walls is consistently too high. It usually has no symptoms, which is why it is called a silent condition, but over time it raises the risk of heart attack, stroke and kidney disease.

## Blood pressure readings
Blood pressure is written as two numbers, systolic over diastolic, for example 120/80 mmHg. A reading of 140/90 mmHg or higher on repeated measurements is generally considered high. A diagnosis should be confirmed by a healthcare professional using several readings, often including home or 24-hour monitoring.

## Causes and risk factors
A diet high in salt, being overweight, lack of exercise, heavy alcohol use, smoking, stress, older age and a family history all raise the risk. Kidney disease and some medicines can also raise blood pressure.

## Lifestyle measures
Reducing salt to less than 5 grams a day (about one teaspoon), eating more fruit, vegetables and whole grains, losing excess weight, exercising regularly, limiting alcohol and stopping smoking can all lower blood pressure.

## Treatment
Many people need daily medicines such as ACE inhibitors, calcium channel blockers or diuretics in addition to lifestyle changes. Blood pressure medicines should not be stopped suddenly without medical advice.

## When to seek urgent help
A very high reading with severe headache, chest pain, confusion, vision changes or shortness of breath is an emergency and needs immediate medical care.

## Related concepts
High blood pressure often occurs together with [type 2 diabetes](/conditions/type2_diabetes.md). A very high reading with severe symptoms is an emergency, see [medical emergency warning signs](/emergency/emergency_warning_signs.md).
