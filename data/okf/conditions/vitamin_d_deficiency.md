## Overview
Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and keeps bones, teeth and muscles healthy. Deficiency is common, including in sunny countries, because of indoor lifestyles, covered clothing, darker skin and air pollution.

## Sources of vitamin D
The skin makes vitamin D when exposed to sunlight. Small amounts come from food such as oily fish, egg yolks, fortified milk and fortified cereals. Supplements are available.

## Symptoms
Many people have no symptoms. When symptoms occur they include tiredness, bone pain, muscle weakness or aches, and frequent infections. In children, severe deficiency causes rickets with bone deformity. In adults it can cause osteomalacia, which is softening of the bones.

## Diagnosis
A blood test measures 25-hydroxyvitamin D. A level below 12 ng/mL (30 nmol/L) is generally considered deficient.

## Treatment and prevention
Treatment is usually a vitamin D supplement at a dose chosen by a doctor, together with sensible sun exposure and a diet that includes vitamin D rich foods. Taking very high doses without supervision can cause too much calcium in the blood and is harmful.

## Who should be tested
People with bone pain, muscle weakness, limited sun exposure, malabsorption conditions, or long-term use of some medicines may be advised to test their levels by a doctor.
