## Overview
Insomnia means difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, together with daytime problems such as tiredness, poor concentration and low mood. Most adults need seven to nine hours of sleep a night.

## Causes
Common causes are stress and worry, an irregular schedule, caffeine or alcohol, screen use late at night, an uncomfortable bedroom, pain, and conditions such as sleep apnea, restless legs, thyroid disease, depression and anxiety. Some medicines also affect sleep.

## Sleep hygiene tips
Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day. Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool. Avoid caffeine after midday and avoid heavy meals, alcohol and screens for an hour or two before bed. Get daylight and exercise during the day, but avoid vigorous exercise right before bedtime. If you cannot sleep after about twenty minutes, get up and do something calming until you feel sleepy.

## Treatment
Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the recommended first-line treatment for long-term insomnia. Sleeping tablets are generally only for short-term use under medical advice because they can cause dependence and drowsiness.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if sleep problems last more than a month, if you snore loudly with pauses in breathing, if you fall asleep during the day, or if low mood or anxiety is affecting you.

## Related concepts
Poor sleep is a common cause of [persistent fatigue](/conditions/persistent_fatigue.md). Thyroid disease can disturb sleep, see [hypothyroidism](/conditions/hypothyroidism.md).
