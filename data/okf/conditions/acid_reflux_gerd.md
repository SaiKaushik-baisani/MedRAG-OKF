## Overview
Acid reflux happens when stomach acid flows back up into the food pipe (oesophagus). When it happens often, it is called gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD).

## Symptoms
The main symptom is heartburn, a burning feeling in the chest behind the breastbone, often after meals or when lying down. Other symptoms include a sour taste in the mouth, regurgitation, a long-lasting cough, hoarseness and difficulty swallowing.

## Triggers
Large or late meals, fatty or spicy foods, coffee, alcohol, smoking, being overweight and lying down soon after eating can trigger reflux. Pregnancy and some medicines can also contribute.

## Lifestyle measures
Eat smaller meals, avoid eating for two to three hours before bed, raise the head of the bed, lose excess weight, stop smoking and avoid personal trigger foods. Loose clothing around the waist may also help.

## Medicines
Antacids give quick, short-term relief. Medicines that reduce stomach acid, such as proton pump inhibitors, are prescribed or recommended by a doctor for frequent symptoms and should be used for the shortest effective time.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if heartburn occurs more than twice a week, if you have trouble swallowing, unintended weight loss, persistent vomiting, black stools or vomiting blood. Chest pain can be a heart problem and should never be assumed to be reflux.
