## Overview
Asthma is a long-term condition in which the airways become inflamed and narrow, making breathing difficult. It affects people of all ages and symptoms can come and go.

## Symptoms
Typical symptoms are wheezing, coughing (often worse at night or early morning), chest tightness and shortness of breath. Symptoms are often triggered by exercise, cold air, dust, pollen, smoke, pollution, strong smells or respiratory infections.

## Triggers and prevention
Avoiding known triggers helps. Keep bedding clean, avoid tobacco smoke, wear a mask during heavy pollution or dust exposure, and treat allergies. Getting the annual flu vaccine is also recommended for people with asthma.

## Treatment
Asthma treatment has two parts. A reliever inhaler, usually containing salbutamol, quickly opens the airways during symptoms. A preventer inhaler containing an inhaled corticosteroid is used regularly to reduce airway inflammation. A doctor creates a personal asthma action plan explaining which inhaler to use and when.

## Asthma attack warning signs
Seek emergency help if the reliever inhaler does not help, if speech is difficult because of breathlessness, if the lips or fingertips turn blue, or if breathing is getting worse quickly. Sit upright and stay calm while waiting for help.

## Follow-up
Regular reviews help make sure the inhaler technique is correct and the treatment is working. Needing a reliever inhaler more than two or three times a week suggests asthma is not well controlled and should be reviewed.
