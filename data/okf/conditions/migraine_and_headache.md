## Overview
Headaches are very common. The most frequent types are tension-type headache and migraine. Most headaches are not caused by a serious disease.

## Tension-type headache
Tension headaches cause a dull, tight, band-like pressure on both sides of the head. They are usually mild to moderate and not made worse by routine activity. Stress, poor posture, lack of sleep and skipping meals are common triggers.

## Migraine
Migraine causes a moderate to severe throbbing pain, often on one side of the head, lasting from four hours up to three days. It is often accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound. Some people experience an aura, such as flashing lights or blind spots, before the headache.

## Common migraine triggers
Triggers include stress, irregular sleep, missed meals, dehydration, bright lights, strong smells, hormonal changes and, for some people, alcohol or certain foods. Keeping a headache diary helps identify personal triggers.

## Self-care and treatment
Rest in a quiet, dark room, drink water and apply a cold or warm compress. Simple pain relievers taken early can help, used as directed on the label. Frequent use of painkillers can itself cause medication-overuse headache. A doctor can prescribe specific migraine treatments and preventive medicine.

## Red flag headaches
Seek urgent medical care for a sudden, severe "worst ever" headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, confusion, weakness, difficulty speaking, vision loss, or a headache after a head injury.
