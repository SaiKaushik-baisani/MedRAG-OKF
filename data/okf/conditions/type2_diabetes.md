## Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body does not use insulin properly, so glucose builds up in the blood. It is the most common form of diabetes and usually develops in adults, although it is increasingly seen in younger people.

## Symptoms
Common symptoms are increased thirst, passing urine often, tiredness, blurred vision, slow healing of cuts, and frequent infections. Many people have no symptoms for years, so the condition is often found on a blood test.

## Risk factors
Being overweight, physical inactivity, a family history of diabetes, increasing age, high blood pressure, and a history of gestational diabetes increase the risk. South Asian populations tend to develop type 2 diabetes at a lower body weight and younger age.

## Diagnosis
Diabetes is diagnosed with a blood test. An HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, or a fasting blood glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, indicates diabetes. An HbA1c between 5.7% and 6.4% suggests prediabetes.

## Management
Management includes a balanced diet with fewer sugary foods and refined carbohydrates, at least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week, weight loss if overweight, and regular blood glucose monitoring. Many people also need medicines such as metformin, and some need insulin. Medicines must only be started or changed by a doctor.

## Complications
Long-term high blood sugar can damage the eyes, kidneys, nerves and blood vessels. Regular eye, foot and kidney checks help find problems early.
