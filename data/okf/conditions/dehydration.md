## Overview
Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in. It is common in hot weather, with fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, heavy sweating during exercise, or when not drinking enough.

## Symptoms
Mild dehydration causes thirst, dry mouth, dark yellow urine, passing less urine, headache, tiredness and dizziness. Severe dehydration can cause very little or no urine, a rapid heartbeat, sunken eyes, confusion and fainting.

## Who is at higher risk
Infants, young children, older adults, people with diabetes and people working or exercising in the heat are at higher risk.

## Treatment
Mild dehydration is treated by drinking fluids regularly in small sips. Oral rehydration solution (ORS) is recommended for diarrhoea and vomiting because it replaces both water and salts. Water, coconut water and clear soups also help. Avoid very sugary drinks and excess caffeine.

## Prevention
Drink fluids throughout the day, more in hot weather or during exercise. Pale yellow urine is a good sign of adequate hydration.

## When to seek medical help
Seek medical care if a person cannot keep fluids down, has severe diarrhoea or vomiting for more than a day, is very drowsy or confused, has had no urine for eight hours or more, or if an infant or older adult shows signs of dehydration.
