## Overview
Iron deficiency anemia happens when the body does not have enough iron to make haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. It is one of the most common nutritional deficiencies worldwide, especially among women and children.

## Symptoms
Typical symptoms are tiredness, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath on exertion, dizziness, headaches, cold hands and feet, brittle nails and a fast heartbeat. Some people crave non-food items such as ice or clay.

## Causes
Causes include a diet low in iron, heavy menstrual periods, pregnancy, blood loss from the digestive tract, and poor iron absorption, for example in coeliac disease. Vegetarians and vegans may need to pay extra attention to iron intake.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis uses a blood test that shows low haemoglobin and low ferritin. A doctor will also look for the reason for iron loss.

## Treatment
Treatment is usually iron supplements taken as prescribed, together with iron-rich foods such as lentils, beans, green leafy vegetables, dates, jaggery, red meat, fish and fortified cereals. Vitamin C, for example from citrus fruit, improves absorption, while tea and coffee taken with meals reduce it. Iron tablets can darken stools and cause constipation or stomach upset.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if you have symptoms of anemia so the cause can be found. Do not self-medicate with iron for long periods, because too much iron is harmful.
