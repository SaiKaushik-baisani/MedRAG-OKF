## Overview
A urinary tract infection (UTI) is an infection of any part of the urinary system. Most UTIs affect the bladder and urethra (lower tract). Women get UTIs more often than men because the female urethra is shorter.

## Symptoms
Common symptoms are a burning feeling when passing urine, needing to pass urine often and urgently, passing small amounts, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, and pain in the lower abdomen. Blood in the urine can occur.

## Kidney infection warning signs
Fever, chills, back or side pain, nausea and vomiting suggest the infection has reached the kidneys. This needs prompt medical treatment.

## Diagnosis and treatment
A doctor may test a urine sample. Bacterial UTIs are treated with antibiotics prescribed by a doctor. The full course should be completed even if symptoms improve. Drinking plenty of water helps flush the urinary tract.

## Prevention
Drink enough fluids, urinate when needed and do not hold urine for long periods, urinate after sexual intercourse, wipe from front to back, and avoid irritating products in the genital area.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor for any suspected UTI, and urgently if there is fever, back pain, vomiting, blood in the urine, or if symptoms occur in pregnancy, in children, or in men.
