## Overview
The common cold and influenza are viral infections of the nose, throat and lungs. They spread through droplets from coughs and sneezes and through contaminated hands and surfaces.

## Common cold
A cold usually starts gradually with a sore throat, runny or blocked nose, sneezing and mild cough. Fever is uncommon in adults. Most colds improve within seven to ten days.

## Influenza
Influenza (flu) usually starts suddenly with fever, chills, body aches, headache, extreme tiredness and dry cough. Symptoms are generally more severe than a cold. Most people recover within one to two weeks, but flu can cause serious illness in older adults, young children, pregnant women and people with chronic conditions.

## Self-care
Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and use saline nasal drops or steam inhalation for a blocked nose. Paracetamol can relieve fever and aches when used according to the label. Antibiotics do not work against viruses and should not be taken for colds or flu unless a doctor finds a bacterial infection.

## Prevention
Wash hands often, cover coughs and sneezes, avoid close contact with sick people and stay home when ill. An annual influenza vaccine reduces the risk of severe illness.

## When to see a doctor
Seek medical advice for difficulty breathing, chest pain, a fever lasting more than three days, symptoms that improve and then suddenly worsen, confusion, or if you are in a high-risk group.

## Related concepts
Fever can lead to fluid loss, see [dehydration](/conditions/dehydration.md). People with [asthma](/conditions/asthma.md) may have more symptoms during respiratory infections.
